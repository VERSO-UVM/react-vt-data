9:T3611,<p><strong>The Challenge of Rural Data: Setting the Stage for the Vermont Data Collaborative </strong></p><p>August 2026</p><p><strong>Introduction</strong> </p><p>In a rural community of 1,000 Vermonters, accurate analysis of population demographics can mean the difference between eligibility for a grant that supports essential infrastructure like building roads or distribution of federal aid. Federal and state data determine who qualifies for what, and rural communities with their smaller proportions of taxpayer funds depend on those programs more than most. Yet rural data is the least reliable: small populations <a href="https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/rural-communities-arent-immune-census-undercount-heres-how-they-can-prepare-2020#:~:text=Why,population">are harder to count</a>, survey <a href="https://www.census.gov/acs/www/methodology/sample-size-and-data-quality/response-rates/">responses are falling</a>, and the local institutions that put the numbers to work are <a href="https://ruralinnovation.us/press-releases/rural-americas-public-investment-gap/">cash-strapped</a> and <a href="https://www.kaxe.org/rural-news/2023-10-26/study-rural-communities-still-at-disadvantage-when-competing-for-govt-grants#:~:text=%E2%80%9CMany,start%2E%E2%80%9D">overburdened</a>. Vermont lives this modern problem and yet, is well positioned to do something about it. </p><p>Access to and use of both <a href="https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/when-federal-data-disappear-so-does-ability-make-effective-policy">Federal</a> and <a href="https://www.pew.org/en/projects/archived-projects/data-as-a-strategic-asset">state</a> data is critical for local stakeholders like state representatives, town officials, public administrators, nonprofits and businesses to support their communities.  </p><p>The challenge of leveraging data in Vermont is not unique. Across the country, rural places share three problems. First, our populations are small enough that standard survey methods produce wider margins of error. Second, our governments are small enough that even good data often goes underused, not for lack of usefulness, but for lack of capacity. Additionally, the systems that house <a href="https://beeckcenter.georgetown.edu/navigating-the-intersection-of-data-sharing-open-data-and-privacy/#:~:text=Common,many%20states">federal</a>, <a href="https://beeckcenter.georgetown.edu/navigating-the-intersection-of-data-sharing-open-data-and-privacy/#:~:text=Common,many%20states">state</a> and municipal data were not designed to be explicitly integrated with one another. Now, there are communities that can turn information into decisions and communities that cannot. </p><p>The University of Vermont is looking to aid Vermonters in better integrating data-informed practices into what makes our communities strong (maybe rework, but we need a transition here) through the Vermont Data Collaborative (VDC). </p><p>The VDC leverages partnerships within and beyond the University of Vermont to create accessible data tools and visualizations that can support data-informed planning and decision making in Vermont communities. Rooted in community engagement, we are building a platform that makes the integration of data in public problem solving easy for folks across the state. </p><p>Read on to learn more about the challenges of using data in rural communities, including Vermont, and the ways UVM is responding to these challenges with new opportunities. </p><p><strong>The Challenge of Federal Data in Rural Communities </strong></p><p>Rural areas are vast and sparsely populated, meaning that traditional data collection practices like by the Census Bureau’s American Community Survey (ACS) program may face challenges when collecting data on rural communities. In rural areas, datasets are more prone to significant <a href="https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/rural-communities-arent-immune-census-undercount-heres-how-they-can-prepare-2020">margins of error </a>and their usability <a href="https://headwaterseconomics.org/economic-development/equity/rural-capacity-map/">suffers from capacity challenges within local governments</a>. Because of small population sizes, slight changes in collection or organization practices can affect important data like town population sizes that impacts congressional spending. In 2020, the U.S. Census Bureau revised their disclosure policies in turn <a href="https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2003714117">creating discrepancies</a> across sectors <a href="https://www.census.gov/acs/www/methodology/sample-size-and-data-quality/response-rates/">in reports for rural and nonwhite populations</a>. As of 2024, the ACS saw response rates decline <a href="https://www.census.gov/acs/www/methodology/sample-size-and-data-quality/response-rates/">from 97% to 83%</a> over a single decade. Fewer responses create more of a challenge for smaller localities, as it widens the margins of error for estimates that allocate services and prove compliance with federal programs. </p><p>The challenges presented by federal data for rural communities do not mean it is not useful. Regardless of how accurate its portrayal of rural areas is, it is one of the most consistent streams of data that is used to develop policy. Importantly, federal data is also used to guide the building of critical infrastructure. In 2021 alone, Census data alone was used to distribute funds for <a href="https://www.census.gov/newsroom/blogs/random-samplings/2023/06/federal-funding-distribution.html">over 350</a> grant programs. The more specific challenge, then, is the fact that the data that contributes to supporting development paints an incomplete picture. There are clear challenges with federal data faced by rural communities. </p><p><strong>The Challenge of State Data in Rural Communities </strong></p><p>State-produced and state-level data are critical to understanding the dynamics of   rural communities.  Leveraging state-level data resources is critically important to crafting a more holistic understanding of the challenges and solutions faced by rural communities in Vermont and beyond and is hindered by two key challenges. </p><p>Capacity challenges </p><p>The most prominent barriers to leveraging state-level data are capacity-based, and has to do with policy compliance, collection practices, and structural issues with smaller datasets. State Chief Information Officers report that state budgets have <a href="https://www.nascio.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/NASCIO_2024-State-CIO-Survey_a11y-1.pdf">not kept up</a> with the increasing demand for digital government services, including those that would collect and leverage data. <a href="https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/waysandmeans/vermonts-outdated-tech-can-leave-lawmakers-in-the-dark/#:~:text=But%20the%20data%20the%20education%20agency%20provided%20were%20%E2%80%9Call%20over%20the%20map%2C%E2%80%9D">Budgetary and regulatory pressures limit states’ ability to experiment with new methodologies, data, and information technology</a> that would allow the data we have to create value for Vermont. </p><p>Vermont’s Agency of Digital Service’s <em>One ADS</em> strategy is built on the understanding that Vermont’s technical systems are <a href="https://legislature.vermont.gov/assets/Legislative-Reports/ADS-2026-Annual-Report_Final.pdf">“fragmented, isolated, and increasingly difficult to maintain.”</a>  Digital infrastructure lays the foundation for the utilization of data. If government officials and community leaders face difficulty accessing digital services that provide access to and visualization of open government data, it reduces the potential of this critical resource. </p><p>Data Challenges</p><p>Recently, Vermont lawmakers have expressed difficulties in using available data, describing what’s available as <a href="https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/waysandmeans/vermonts-outdated-tech-can-leave-lawmakers-in-the-dark/#:~:text=The%20data%20that%20state%20agencies%20release%20are%20frequently%20incomplete%20or%20inaccurate%2E%20Outdated%20information%20technology%20systems%20often%20are%20to%20blame">“frequently incomplete or inaccurate”</a> according to <em>Seven Days</em>. Sen. Martine Gulick described the data as <a href="https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/waysandmeans/vermonts-outdated-tech-can-leave-lawmakers-in-the-dark/#:~:text=But%20the%20data%20the%20education%20agency%20provided%20were%20%E2%80%9Call%20over%20the%20map%2C%E2%80%9D">“all over the map,”</a> and when emphasizing the importance of data to lawmaking, noting that “If we don’t have the right data, <a href="https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/waysandmeans/vermonts-outdated-tech-can-leave-lawmakers-in-the-dark/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CData%20is%20incredibly%20important%20to%20everything%20we%20do%20in%20the%20legislature%2C%E2%80%9D%20Gulick%20said%2E%20%E2%80%9CIf%20we%20don%E2%80%99t%20have%20the%20right%20data%2C%20the%20consequences%20could%20be%20devastating%2E%E2%80%9D">the consequences could be devastating.”</a> </p><p>States managing their own data face their <a href="https://beeckcenter.georgetown.edu/navigating-the-intersection-of-data-sharing-open-data-and-privacy/#:~:text=Common,many%20states">own isolated problems</a>. Their data is often not standardized in format or quality, and rarely do avenues exist for agencies to collaborate and share data with others. Additionally, <a href="https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/how-state-data-systems-shape-federal-statistics/">state-produced data are integrated into federal statistics</a>, meaning that any inaccurate data in a state’s analyses has the potential to scale when used. </p><p>Data for things like policymaking, development, public services, agriculture, and distribution of state funds are difficult to access and utilize in their current format without capacity and technical expertise, something that <a href="https://outside.vermont.gov/agency/ACCD/ACCD_Web_Docs/CD/CPR/Current%20Initiatives/DHCD-UVM-VERTA-2025-FINALREPORT.pdf?_gl=1*211l0t*_ga*ODY1MDg0MTYyLjE3MzgxMDYyMzg.*_ga_V9WQH77KLW*czE3ODQ2NTgxMzMkbzEwMCRnMSR0MTc4NDY1ODE2NyRqMjYkbDAkaDA.">Vermont’s rural communities often lack</a>. </p><p><strong>The Challenge of Capacity </strong></p><p>Rural areas face a <a href="https://ruralinnovation.us/press-releases/rural-americas-public-investment-gap/">strain on public resources</a>. Common issues like smaller tax bases, limited staffing, less access to flexible funding, and higher per-capita public spending construct barriers to <a href="https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/how-improve-evidence-drive-rural-policy-and-investments">evaluating</a> and supporting basic public services, <a href="https://ruralorganizing.org/blog/new-memo-the-federal-budget-and-its-effects-on-rural-communities/">rural resources are stretched thin</a>, and people are <a href="https://www.ruralhealth.us/blogs/2025/04/critical-condition-how-medicaid-cuts-would-reshape-rural-health-care-landscapes">directly affected</a>. </p><p>When a town can’t tell whether a policy worked, it can’t make the case to its people to continue, expand, or replace it, and it certainly can’t make that case to another funder or the federal government. Over time, <a href="https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/waysandmeans/vermonts-outdated-tech-can-leave-lawmakers-in-the-dark/">communities that can measure their results adapt faster</a> than communities that cannot. </p><p><strong>Conclusion</strong></p><p>Taken together, the federal, state and capacity challenges identified here point to a problem with data from three directions: 1) at the federal level, datasets that guide critical funding and program eligibility are not always reliable for rural areas; 2) at the state level, data is often unstandardized, difficult to share across agencies, and constrained by budget and infrastructure; 3) finally, at the local level, limited staff and resources make it hard to utilize data even when it exists. The resulting environment is one that exhibits a measurable difference in how well communities can translate information into decisions. Vermont encounters all these challenges directly. Addressing them requires making public data more accessible, integrated, and usable for the communities that depend on it. </p><p>In rural communities, the path ahead seeks to use federal data to contextualize challenges, while amplifying other important data sources to generate a more holistic understanding of rural communities. </p><p>The University of Vermont is exploring ways to bolster capacity and make data more accessible and useful for communities across the state. The Vermont Data Collaborative is an open-source data access and visualization platform that seeks to serve Vermont, enabling community leaders to make more informed decisions for their community. </p><p>This article is the first in a series of articles documenting the challenges that Vermont’s communities face in using data, and how an open data project such as the Vermont Data Collaborative may fit into building a more holistic and accessible data ecosystem for Vermont. </p><p>To learn more about the Vermont Data Collaborative and how your community or organization might benefit from its use, please contact … to schedule a personalized presentation where we will showcase the Vermont Data Collaborative and curate its tools for your specific needs. </p><p><em>Next up: If rural data presents so many challenges, where do we go from here?
